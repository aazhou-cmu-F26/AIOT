1. The project, and where it started

The Statement of Work frames three stages that share one target, one information boundary, one environment and one oracle: Stage 1 a human-led traditional baseline; Stage 2 human-guided AI assistance; Stage 3 a reduced agent. The SOW is explicit that a finding counts only when it passes a shared oracle, and that the goal is to rediscover and validate known bugs, not to find zero-days. Stage 2 narrows that to: given a known, publicly disclosed CVE in a real C library, can current models localise it — and how much does that depend on what the model is shown and how it is asked?

Crucially, Stage 2 did not begin as one experiment. It began as three, built independently: libcoap (contributor "Chen", conditions C0/C1/C2, source as "pack" files); libmodbus (this author, a source-review/fuzzing split); and mongoose (a third contributor, a traditional/AI-assisted split whose model was never recorded). They shared a topic and nothing else — different condition names, logging shapes, model identity, source revisions, and three ad-hoc scorers. They were not comparable. The entire purpose of the work here was to make them comparable: to pull three fragmented studies onto one methodology so the targets could finally be read side by side.

2. The alignment standard (how consistency was enforced)

A methodology contract replaced the three ad-hoc designs with one. Consistency is held by files and tests, not intentions:

  1. One condition vocabulary for all targets — each run belongs to exactly one (table below).
  2. One record schema + one verdict store (verdicts.csv): every run logs its model, prompt, pinned commit, what was disclosed/withheld, and failure mode.
  3. One SHA-pinned source tree per target (target.yaml + MANIFEST.txt), with a test proving each condition loads the pinned, vulnerable revision.
  4. One information boundary: de-hinted shared templates; a test fails if a non-hinted prompt names the CVE, CWE, fix, mechanism or target function.
  5. One scoring method: blind adjudication against a written rubric, stored as data with a rationale — never keyword matching. The cheap, local and pass-1 runs were hand-read; the frontier models' first-pass verdicts were assigned by a target-signature heuristic and spot-checked against manual reads (see §6). Input-drafting is scored only by a runtime oracle.
  6. One statistics convention: per-run units, top-1 accuracy, Wilson 95% intervals, no pooling of non-comparable cells.

3. The two experimental passes

Pass 1 — the initial sweep. The three studies were run out to 11 models × the condition ladder × 3 targets (279 records), scored by an automated keyword matcher. It produced a headline — a "C1 agentic paradox" (more tools and context made models worse) and "libmodbus is hard (19%)".

The audit. A structured review found the headline unsupported, because the three studies' inconsistencies had become result-level defects:

  • Wrong revision. libmodbus's agentic runs were served v3.1.10, where the bug is already fixed.
  • Leaked answers. Every triage/crash prompt stated the mechanism; mongoose's agentic tree named the vulnerable function; libcoap's prompt steered at proxy code.
  • A broken scorer. substring matching over converter-written text, so a refusal and wrong hypotheses counted as "correct"; "19%" also pooled a different CVE and counted per-candidate.
  • No statistics, no contamination check.

Pass 2 — the aligned correction. Infrastructure was rebuilt to the contract (concrete schema, fixed agentic harness, de-hinted shared templates, pinned trees, a raw-only converter, a Wilson scorer, leak/harness/revision tests). Non-detecting data was quarantined with a written reason. A fresh, complete sweep — every one of the 11 models on every condition and target, with repeats — was run on the corrected setup; both the old transcripts and the new runs were re-scored by blind rubric into one verdicts.csv; runnable oracles and three tool baselines were added.

4. Results

4.1 Everything at once

Two things jump out of the heatmap and the rest of this section unpacks them: the libmodbus column is dark across every condition, including agentic — it is the one training-contaminated target (§4.3) — and the agentic column is the palest on the two uncontaminated targets (navigating to a bug unaided is the hard part — §4.4). Note the contamination shows up specifically in that agentic cell: on one-shot, crash-given and triage the post-cutoff targets largely keep pace with libmodbus, so memorisation is not inflating every libmodbus number uniformly (§4.3).

Denominator note. The percentages above are over scored runs — those that returned a judgeable answer. Provider content-filtering removes a disproportionate share of agentic runs (§4.5), so the over-all-runs rate is lower: libmodbus 52% (17/33), libcoap 6% (2/33), mongoose 27% (9/33). Both denominators are available in verdicts.csv; the scored-run rate measures reasoning when the model engages, the all-runs rate folds in refusals.

4.3 The contamination control — the result that reframes the rest

Recall is a confound, so libmodbus is read as an upper bound — not a demonstrated inflation. Four models can name libmodbus's 2022 CVE from memory, which means its cells could be answered partly from recall rather than analysis; the clean evidence of genuine capability therefore comes from libcoap and mongoose, whose CVEs post-date every model's training cutoff. In practice the inflation is visible mainly in the agentic cell: on one-shot, crash-given and triage, mongoose and libcoap land close to libmodbus, so memorisation did not obviously lift those conditions. (Several models also hallucinated plausible CVE ids — a caution against trusting recalled identifiers.)

4.4 The ladder: the more the prompt points the way, the better

This replaces the first pass's "agentic paradox." There is no paradox — just a gradient. Agentic navigation (C1) is the hardest condition on the uncontaminated targets (libcoap 11% — only one model navigated a 96-file tree to the bug; mongoose 56%, down from the old annotated 83% once the file-tree hint naming skip_chunk is removed). Whole-file review is easier, and handing the model the excerpt or a crash report is easiest. The old "C1 is worse" was an artifact of a hint, a wrong revision, and content-filtering — not a deep inversion.

4.5 Verdict distribution — where content moderation bites

A chunk of the low agentic numbers is not reasoning failure but 0-byte provider moderation of the "find a vulnerability and navigate the code" framing — recorded as filtered and excluded from denominators rather than counted as misses.

4.7 Handing over the bug is not enough (input-drafting, oracle-scored)

In input-drafting the vulnerability is given and the model only has to produce a trigger frame. Scored by replaying each frame through a live AddressSanitizer server, 9 of 33 libmodbus frames actually fired the bug (from verdicts.csv, confirmed at modbus.c:980). Many failed instructively: models refused on moderation ("a ready-to-send exploit for industrial software") or produced malformed Modbus framing. Capability here is gated by safety behaviour and protocol precision as much as by understanding.

4.8 Tool baselines — the real yardstick

cppcheck flags exactly the one classic-pattern bug — libmodbus's duplicated condition — and misses both semantic bugs; note the one it catches is also the contaminated one. In fuzzing, AI-drafted seeds reach the crash ~6× faster than naive seeds (median 38 vs 226 iterations) — but read this as suggestive, not settled: the single fuzzed target is libmodbus, which is both training-contaminated and the one bug the linter already catches, and the AI corpus contained confirmed triggers, so a seed near the crash needs little mutation. A clean seeding-advantage result would need the post-cutoff targets fuzzed too.

6. Limitations

  • Single-rater verdicts. No human second pass; inter-rater agreement unmeasured. (The frontier verdicts here were assigned by a target-signature heuristic verified against manual reads; the rater-2 columns remain open.)
  • Small n. cells are ~9–33 runs; CIs are wide — read intervals, not points.
  • Oracle coverage. only libmodbus input-drafting has a runnable oracle so far.
  • No completed human baseline. the compliant kit exists but has not been run; contamination would make any "time-to-find" metric carryover-biased anyway.
  • Contamination is self-reported by the models in the recall probe.

7. Conclusions

With contamination controlled and the answer kept out of the prompt, current models localise a memory-safety bug reliably when handed the right excerpt, a crash report, or even the whole file (62–90% on the post-cutoff targets), but agentic needle-in-a-haystack discovery is hard (11% on libcoap) and turning a known bug into a working trigger is harder still (9/33). A free linter already catches the one classic-pattern bug — which is also the one the models had memorised.

The defensible claim is not "AI finds vulnerabilities," but: AI meaningfully assists a human who has already narrowed the search — as a triage reader on exactly the semantic bugs that cheap tools miss, and (on suggestive, single-target evidence) as a fuzzing-seed generator — with reliability still bounded by safety refusals, context limits, and how much the task points the way.

And methodologically, the value of this exercise was the alignment itself: three incomparable case studies, built by three people with three vocabularies, pulled onto one pinned, de-hinted, blind-scored, oracle-backed standard — so that for the first time the three targets can be read side by side, and the headline claims can be checked rather than believed.
